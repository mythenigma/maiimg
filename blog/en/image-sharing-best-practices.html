Best Practices

A good image share is not just a URL. It has a clear audience, a clear purpose, a reasonable file count, and an ending. These practices keep photo links easier to open and easier to manage.

Practical flow

Scope beats volume

A smaller, clearer share is usually better than a large dump.

Context helps recipients

Label the link so people know what they are opening.

Controls reduce cleanup

Set the limit before sending, not after the link has spread.

How the share should work

Scope

Make the link answer one question

A share for family, a buyer, a listing, or an event should each have its own focus. If the audience changes, make a new share instead of stretching one link too far.

Channels

Use links and QR codes intentionally

The direct link is best for chat, email, and documents. The QR code is best for posters, screens, printed notes, or in-person handoff.

Controls

Close links when their purpose ends

Use view limits, expiry, or revoke when the share is temporary. Old links should not remain open just because nobody remembered to close them.

Checklist

  • Use one link for one purpose.
  • Keep the set under 25 files.
  • Put the strongest or most useful image first.
  • Label the share before sending it.
  • Use QR for physical or scan-based contexts.
  • Set expiry or revoke old shares when done.
